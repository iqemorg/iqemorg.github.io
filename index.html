Institute of Quantum Engineering in Medicine

The Institute of Quantum Engineering in Medicine (IQEM) is an open-access research organization dedicated to advancing the science and engineering of photomedicine. IQEM studies how light can be engineered as a biological input to produce controlled therapeutic effects. IQEM is committed to ensuring that all research, discoveries, and technologies are freely accessible and remain in the public domain.​

Photomedicine from First Principles

Light therapies work via the absorption of quantum energy. Quantum energy refers to the discrete packets of energy carried by photons, the fundamental particles of light.

The quantum energy of a photon is given by E = h c λ where h is Planck’s constant, c is the speed of light, and λ is the photon’s wavelength.1

  • The biological effects of light are due to each photon's quantum energy, which is quantified by its wavelength.
  • Light interacts with biological cells, including the mitochondria, helping them generate cellular fuel (ATP) and repair damage.2
  • Therapeutic wavelengths span the ultraviolet, visible, and infrared ranges.
  • In medicine, these physics principles are key to the development of non-invasive light therapies.

Landmark Evidence

  • The Nobel Prize in Medicine was awarded to Dr. Niels R. Finsen in 1903 for pioneering light therapies: ultraviolet light for tuberculosis3 and red light for smallpox.4
  • Harvard Medical School researchers have published numerous peer-reviewed studies on the effectiveness of red and infrared light therapies, popularizing the term photobiomodulation.2
  • NASA researchers developed LED-based photobiomodulation systems to accelerate tissue regeneration and wound healing.5
  • Human clinical trials have successfully proven several photobiomodulation treatments.6

Slices of Sunlight ☀️

  • UV-B light is crucial for naturally synthesizing vitamin D, a process vital for the immune system.7
  • UV-A light triggers the release of nitric oxide (NO), enhancing cardiovascular function and reducing blood pressure.8
  • Red light stimulates mitochondrial activity, increases energy production (ATP), and reduces oxidative stress.9
    • Red light is widely used for pain relief10, skin rejuvenation11, and muscle recovery.12
  • Near-Infrared (NIR) wavelengths penetrate deeply into tissues to relieve pain, reduce inflammation, and treat neurological conditions.13,14
    • Clinical trials have successfully treated chronic pain15, depression16, anxiety17, and brain neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's18, Parkinson's19).

Sunlight is Medicine

  • Regular sunlight exposure significantly reduces all-cause mortality20, cardiovascular disease21, and multiple cancer types (15+) in population studies.22
  • Bright light therapy treats depression23, improves sleep quality24, and regulates circadian rhythms25 with natural daylight cycles.
  • Sunlight enhances cognitive function and memory consolidation by increasing brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) levels.26
  • Moderate UV exposure provides protective effects against autoimmune diseases (rheumatoid arthritis27, inflammatory bowel disease28, type 1 diabetes29).
